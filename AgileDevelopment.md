# SpaceX Starship Rocket Launches (1–5): Agile Development

The video showcases SpaceX's Starship rocket launches 1 through 5, demonstrating **Agile Development** in action.
This is hardware and more dramatic. We have a massive advantage in software because we can "launch the rocket" and see what happens for free.



---

## Launch 1
* Several engines failed on launch.
* The rocket failed to separate.
* The rocket experienced a **"Rapid Unscheduled Disassembly" (RUD)**.
* The launch pad was destroyed by the sheer power of liftoff (despite the missing engines).

## Launch 2
* All engines worked properly.
* The rocket separated successfully.
* SpaceX introduced **"hot staging,"** where the upper stage fires before separation.
* The first stage experienced an RUD, followed shortly by the second stage experiencing an RUD.
* The launch pad was upgraded with a **"water deluge system"** to prevent the massive thrust from destroying it again.

## Launch 3
* The rocket separated, and the first stage executed a **"boost back burn."**
* The second stage reached space and entered a coast phase.
* The first stage crashed into the sea.
* The second stage entered an uncontrollable roll and burned up upon re-entry.

## Launch 4
* *(Note: One engine was out on the first stage during launch).*
* The rocket executed a boost back burn and successfully hovered over the sea before sinking.
* The launch tower executed test movements to mimic catching the hovering rocket.
* The second stage began re-entry, and plasma started to burn through the control surfaces.
* Despite the damage, the onboard software adapted, and the second stage successfully hovered over the ocean.

## Launch 5
* The first stage returned to the launch site, hovered, and was **caught by the launch tower** *(widely considered the most significant engineering achievement since the Moon landings, despite many initially thinking the idea was a joke when proposed)*.
* The second stage experienced some burn-through on its control surfaces.
* The second stage successfully hovered over the ocean *(catching the second stage is likely to happen in the first half of 2026)*.

### List of Launches
| Flight | Date | Key Highlights & Milestones |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **IFT-1** | 20 Apr 2023 | Maiden full-stack launch; pad damage and vehicle breakup during ascent. |
| **IFT-2** | 18 Nov 2023 | Debut of hot-staging; booster lost after stage separation, ship lost near ascent end. |
| **IFT-3** | 14 Mar 2024 | Reached suborbital space; demonstrated payload door opening and propellant transfer; ship disintegrated on reentry. |
| **IFT-4** | 6 Jun 2024 | First controlled booster and ship splashdowns; ship survived reentry. |
| **IFT-5** | 13 Oct 2024 | First Super Heavy booster catch by the launch tower; controlled ship splashdown. |
| **IFT-6** | 19 Nov 2024 | Booster catch aborted/diverted to splashdown; ship completed reentry and splashdown. |
| **IFT-7** | 16 Jan 2025 | Block 2 debut; second booster tower catch; ship lost on ascent due to leak. |
| **IFT-8** | 6 Mar 2025 | Third booster tower catch; ship lost during ascent/flight. |
| **IFT-9** | 27 May 2025 | First reflown booster (Booster 14); booster and ship lost during mission. |
| **IFT-10** | 26 Aug 2025 | Successful suborbital flight test and splashdowns. |
| **IFT-11** | 13 Oct 2025 | Final Block 2 flight; successful test flight and splashdowns. |
| **IFT-12** | 22 May 2026 | Block 3 debut and first launch from Orbital Pad 2; booster failed landing burn, ship completed reentry/splashdown. |
| **IFT-13** | 24 Jul 2026 | Deployed first operational Starlink satellites; booster lost on landing burn, ship splashed down intact. |
| **IFT-14** | 28 Sep 2026 | First Starship flight to reach Earth orbit; deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites. |
| **IFT-15** | 13 Nov 2026 | Deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites; Ship 42 caught by launch tower on 14 Nov 2026 (first ship catch). |
